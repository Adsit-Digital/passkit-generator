## 1. Naming

### 1.1 Candidates evaluated

Five candidates were generated and each was checked via live web search for collisions in restaurant tech, wallet passes, and loyalty. Findings below are from research conducted July 2026 (sources in Appendix A).

| # | Name | The idea | How it sounds when a server says it | Collision risk (what we found) | Domain outlook | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Currant** | Double meaning: a small fruit (food-warm, garnish-scale) + a pun on *current* — offers that are always up to date on the lock screen. Encodes pillars 3 and 4 in one word. | "Scan that QR — it's our Currant coupon, it goes right into your phone's wallet." Diner hears "current," which is harmless and even reinforcing. | **Low, all out-of-category.** Currant Inc. — a small smart-home energy startup (4 employees, appears inactive); CurrantNOW — a disaster-response app concept; Currant Tech — a tiny consultancy; Social Currant — a social-media agency; currant.it — a UK web-design shop; CurrantC — a berry farm; Currant American Brasserie — a single San Diego restaurant (Class 43 restaurant services, not software). **Nothing in restaurant tech, wallet passes, or loyalty.** | `currant.com` is not held by any major player (no live site surfaced in search) but assume it's parked/priced. Established trademark use in exactly our category. | Irrelevant given collisions; spelling alone (lagniappe/lanyap/laniappe) would bleed word-of-mouth traffic. | ❌ Eliminated |

### 1.2 Recommendation: Currant

**Currant wins because it is the only candidate that is simultaneously clean and meaningful.** Every other candidate with a great metaphor (Chalkboard, Blue Plate, Lagniappe) turned out to have a live, in-category or same-buyer collision; Currant's collisions are a dead smart-home startup, a berry farm, and a web-design shop in Coventry — none in restaurant tech, loyalty, or wallet passes, and none with the scale to contest a mark in our classes (software/marketing services, Classes 9/35/42). Meanwhile the name quietly encodes our two hardest-to-explain differentiators: offers that are always *current* (push-updated, lock-screen live) and updated in seconds. It's food, not tech — a currant is garnish-scale, warm, and a little tart, which is exactly the personality. It contains no "pass," "wallet," "loyal," or "-ly," so it can't be confused with PassKit, Walletly, Passcreator, PassSlot, or the Loyalty-* crowd, and nothing about it whispers blockchain. The homophone with "current" is the one real risk (someone typing "current app" won't find us), and we mitigate it the way every fruit-named company does: always pair the name with the wordmark and berry, buy the obvious misspelling domains if cheap, and let "Currant — like the berry, and like your offers" be the one-line explanation reps and founders repeat. Recommended primary domain: **getcurrant.com**, with **currant.app** or **usecurrant.com** as fallbacks; pursue currant.com opportunistically later.

### 1.3 Name usage rules

- Always **Currant** — capital C, never "CurrantApp," "Currant.io," or "the Currant app" in headlines (in body copy "the Currant dashboard" is fine).
- Never spell it "Current." In legal and support docs, first mention may read: *Currant (yes, like the berry)*.
- "Wallet" always means Apple Wallet or Google Wallet. Never use "wallet" unqualified in a sentence where it could read as crypto ("connect your wallet" is banned phrasing, forever).

## 2. Positioning

### 2.1 Positioning statement (one sentence)

> For independent restaurants that can't afford app-based loyalty suites, **Currant** is the coupon platform that delivers offers straight into diners' Apple and Google Wallets — no app to install, no email to collect — and keeps every offer current on the lock screen.

### 2.2 Tagline (primary + alternates)

- **Primary:** *Your offer, on their lock screen.* (6 words)
- Alternate A (privacy-forward): *No app. No email. Just offers.*
- Alternate B (name-forward): *Coupons that stay current.*

### 2.3 Boilerplate (≈50 words)

> Currant helps independent restaurants turn a QR scan into a coupon that lives in the diner's Apple or Google Wallet. No app to install, no email to collect — offers sit on the lock screen and stay current with every update. Self-serve, from $29 a month per location.

## 3. Voice & tone

Currant sounds like **a good host**: confident because the food is good, warm because you're welcome here, brief because your table is ready. Never a martech vendor, never a crypto bro, never breathless.

### Principle 1 — Host, not vendor

Speak to restaurants as a peer who works the floor, not a platform that "empowers SMBs."

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| "Your Tuesday special, on every regular's phone." | "Leverage omnichannel wallet infrastructure to drive guest engagement." |
| Dashboard greeting: "Welcome back. Two coupons live, 41 redemptions this week." | "Your engagement KPIs are trending positively!" |

### Principle 2 — Say the plain thing

Use the words a server would use across the counter: *coupon, offer, scan, wallet, redeem*. Never *incentive asset, engagement unit, unlock, activate*.

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Button: **Create a coupon** | Button: "Launch campaign" |
| Button: **Update the offer** | Button: "Push engagement update" |
| "Staff scan the pass to redeem it." | "Frontline associates validate the credential." |

### Principle 3 — Make the privacy promise out loud, every time

"We never ask for their email" is our sharpest differentiator. It appears wherever a diner or restaurant might expect a data grab — and we never undercut it with sneaky exceptions.

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Claim page fine print: "**{Restaurant} never sees your name, email, or number.** The coupon just lives on your phone." | A "subscribe for more offers" email field anywhere in the diner flow. |
| Dashboard: "You have 214 passes in wallets. You don't know who they are — that's the point." | "Enrich guest profiles with first-party data." |

### Principle 4 — Confident, never breathless

No hype words: *revolutionize, game-changing, supercharge, unleash, 10x, magic*. Numbers and time do the bragging: *60 seconds, $29, zero emails collected*.

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| "Change the offer in 60 seconds. It updates on every phone." | "Revolutionary real-time pass technology!" |
| Empty state: "No coupons yet. Your first one takes about a minute." | "Get ready to transform your guest journey! 🚀" |

### Principle 5 — Short enough to say across the counter

Every diner-facing string must survive being read aloud by a busy server over kitchen noise. If a sentence can't be spoken in one breath, cut it.

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Table tent: "Scan for a free dessert — goes right in your wallet." | "Scan this QR code to access exclusive personalized savings opportunities." |
| Post-add toast: "It's in your wallet. See you soon." | "Success! Your digital pass has been provisioned to your device." |

### Microcopy reference strings

| Surface | String |
|---|---|
| Primary dashboard CTA | `Create a coupon` |
| Edit action | `Update the offer` |
| Push confirmation | `Updated. Every pass in a wallet gets the new offer.` |
| Empty state (coupons) | `No coupons yet. Your first one takes about a minute.` |
| Empty state (redemptions) | `No redemptions yet. Put the QR where people wait — table tents and receipts work best.` |
| Delete confirm | `End this offer? Passes already in wallets will show it as expired.` |

| Diner claim page subhead | `Add it to your wallet and it's yours. No app, no email, no signup.` |
| Wallet buttons | Use the **official** Apple "Add to Apple Wallet" and Google "Save to Google Wallet" badges — never restyle or reword them. |
| Post-add confirmation | `It's in your wallet. We'll keep it up to date — see you soon.` |
| Diner privacy line | `{Restaurant name} never sees your name, email, or number.` |

## 4. Visual identity

### 4.1 Color palette

Warm, food-first, and built to look good both in a sunlit dining room (light) and on a lock screen at night (dark). Deliberately **no SaaS teal, no crypto purple**: the primary is held at a red-burgundy hue (~350°) — wine and berry, not blockchain. All light-theme text/background pairs listed as "text" meet WCAG AA (≥4.5:1) on their stated surface.

| Token | Role | Light theme | Dark theme | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `currant` | Primary brand / primary buttons / links | `#7D2A3A` | `#E08D9B` | 8.4:1 on Cream (light); 7:1 on Char (dark). The pass background color. |
| `copper` | Accent — highlights, badges, hover, label text on currant | `#A8541F` | `#E29B6A` | Warm metal of the pass rail and bar tops. AA as text on Cream. |
| `cream` | App background / pass foreground | `#FAF5EC` | `#1B1512` (Char, bg) | Paper menu, not clinical white. Dark theme background is warm near-black, not gray. |
| `ink` | Primary text | `#241E19` | `#F2E9DD` | Warm near-black; never pure `#000`. |
| `oat` | Secondary text, borders, dividers | `#6E6259` | `#A99C8F` | 5.4:1 on Cream — safe for secondary text. |
| `herb` | Success (redemption confirmed, pass added) | `#3E7A4C` | `#86C29A` | Herb green, not terminal green. |
| `honey` | Warning (offer expiring, quota near) | `#96610D` | `#E4B45A` | Honey amber, not alarm orange. |

Surface note: dark-theme cards/surfaces sit one step above background at `#262019`.

```css

}
:root[data-theme="dark"] {
  --currant: #E08D9B; --copper: #E29B6A;
  --ink: #F2E9DD;     --oat: #A99C8F;
  --herb: #86C29A;    --honey: #E4B45A;
  --bg: #1B1512;      --surface: #262019; --text: var(--ink);
}
```

### 4.2 Typography (no webfonts in MVP)

System stacks only — zero font payload, native rendering on every device, and the serif display gives us hospitality warmth no default-SaaS Inter clone has.

| Role | Stack | Weights | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Display** | `ui-serif, "New York", "Iowan Old Style", Georgia, Cambria, "Times New Roman", serif` | 600, 700 | Marketing headlines, claim-page headline, dashboard page titles. Sizes 24–40px, line-height 1.15, slight negative tracking at 32px+. |
| **Body / UI** | `-apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Segoe UI", Roboto, "Helvetica Neue", Arial, "Noto Sans", sans-serif` | 400, 500, 600 | Everything interactive: buttons, forms, tables, body copy. Base 16px marketing / 15px dashboard, line-height 1.5. |
| **Data** | `ui-monospace, "SF Mono", SFMono-Regular, Menlo, Consolas, "Liberation Mono", monospace` | 400, 500 | Redemption codes, short links, counts and analytics figures. Always set `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on stat displays (also on Body when showing numbers in tables). |

Rule of thumb: serif speaks, sans works, mono counts.

### 4.3 Logo / mark direction (two concepts, words only)

**Concept A — "The Sprig."** A minimal sprig of three currant berries on one curved stem, berries descending in size — reading simultaneously as fruit, as a trio of notification dots, and as a gentle downward motion into a pocket. Drawn in single-weight rounded strokes, Currant red on Cream. Wordmark "Currant" set in the display serif, sprig hanging from the final *t* like a garnish on a glass rim. **29×29px degradation:** drop the stem and two berries; a single solid currant-red circle with one small cream highlight dot, on a cream rounded-square tile. At pass-icon size it must read as "a berry," nothing more.

**Concept B — "The Clipped Corner."** A rounded-rectangle card (the pass itself) with a half-circle notch clipped from the top-right corner — the coupon-scissors cut and the ticket punch in one gesture — with a single berry dot nested in the notch. Currant-red tile, cream notch and dot. Flexible as a container: the tile can hold a restaurant's initial in co-branded contexts. **29×29px degradation:** the tile shape with notch survives perfectly at small sizes; drop the berry dot below 20px and keep only tile + notch.

Direction: prototype A first (warmer, more ownable); B is the fallback if A reads as generic fruit at favicon size.

### 4.4 Wallet pass defaults (default coupon template)

These are the shipped defaults for restaurants that haven't customized. All three meet legibility contrast on a lock screen.

| PassKit field | Value | Hex equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| `backgroundColor` | `rgb(125, 42, 58)` | `#7D2A3A` |
| `foregroundColor` | `rgb(250, 245, 236)` | `#FAF5EC` |
| `labelColor` | `rgb(237, 176, 136)` | `#EDB088` |

**Guidance for restaurants choosing their own colors** (surface this in the dashboard color picker):

1. **Dark background, light text.** Passes live on lock screens, often at night. A deep color from your awning, menu, or bar looks like your restaurant; white looks like a default.
2. Keep foreground-on-background contrast **≥ 4.5:1** — the picker should compute this live and block combinations below it (show: "Hard to read on a lock screen").
3. `labelColor` must differ visibly from `foregroundColor` (labels are the small captions like "EXPIRES"); suggest a tint of the background hue or a warm metallic.
4. Avoid pure black (`rgb(0,0,0)` reads as a boarding pass) and avoid red-on-green or other colorblind-hostile pairs.
5. Preview every choice on the built-in lock-screen mock — both iOS light and dark wallpapers — before saving.

## 5. Applied copy

### 5.1 Landing page hero

- **Headline:** `Turn one scan into a regular.`
- **Subhead:** `Currant puts your coupon in your diners' Apple or Google Wallet — no app for them to install, no email for you to collect — and lets you change the offer in 60 seconds.`
- **Primary CTA:** `Create your first coupon — free`
- **Secondary CTA:** `See a live pass demo`

### 5.2 Value-prop blocks

**1. No app. No email. No friction.**
Diners scan a QR on the table, tap once, and your offer is in their wallet — that's the whole flow. You never ask for their email or number, and they never forget where the coupon went.

**2. Live on their lock screen.**
A pass in the wallet isn't a flyer in the trash — it's on the phone they check 100 times a day. Change your offer and every pass updates itself, so Tuesday's special is never advertising last Tuesday.

**3. Sixty seconds, twenty-nine dollars.**
Write the offer, pick the color, print the QR — you're live before the lunch rush. From $29 a month per location, self-serve, no POS contract and no sales call required.

### 5.3 Diner-facing claim page

- **Headline:** `{Offer name}, from {Restaurant name}.` — e.g., *A free dessert, from Nonna's.*
- **Subhead:** `Add it to your wallet and it's yours. No app, no email, no signup.`
- **Buttons:** official **Add to Apple Wallet** / **Save to Google Wallet** badges (platform artwork, unmodified; show the badge matching the diner's device first).
- **Post-add confirmation:** `It's in your wallet. We'll keep it up to date — see you soon.`
- **Fine print:** `{Restaurant name} never sees your name, email, or number.`

### 5.4 Table-tent one-liners (pick per offer)

- `Scan for a free dessert — it goes right in your wallet. No app, no email.`
- `Tonight's deal lives on your lock screen. Scan to grab it.`
- `Regulars get the good stuff. Scan once — we'll keep your coupon current.`
